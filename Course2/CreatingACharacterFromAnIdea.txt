Paul Atreides is a man obsessed; he needs to escape the terrible future he sees in his prescient visions. While his actions cause the dystopian future, he wants to save humanity and have a single true choice of his own. Chani, his lover and friend, requires a stable life with her lover; one where they can be honest and uncontrolled by prophesy, politics and the tyranny of an empire. While their love is real, Paul often causes a separation between them due to his visions and duties.

Scene:

The palace terrace overlooked the city of Arrakeen. A breeze blew sending spirals of sand along the white walls.

Paul sat in the shade of an awning. Chani was placing the last of the spice coffee into the three tiny cups. She passed one to Paul and one to the stranger.

“She carried messages between the southern sietches during the last water dispute.”

“And I carried them faster than any man who doubted me.” She raised her cup and smiled.

Chani let out a soft laughter that warmed Paul’s heart.

"You have a dangerous smile," Chani said.

“Usually by men who deserved it,” Sira said.

Paul tilted his head back to where her voice was coming from.

“I speak as if fear is a poor host.”

"No, I talk like I don’t want you to be afraid."

Chani’s hand brushed against Sira’s as she reached for the sugar. It was unintentional but Paul could see a thousand possible futures in that moment. Chani laughing next to another woman, Chani leaving the palace behind and Chani looking at him with the same gentle gaze she used to save for him.

Paul gripped his cup tighter.

“You are very silent, Muad’Dib.”

"It’s a useful trait."

"If you have something to hide."

Chani shot Paul a look.

"Sira says the emperor should spend one evening speaking like an ordinary man."

“An ordinary man has fewer enemies.”

"And fewer excuses."

There was a pause. The breeze blew across the terrace, bringing with it a smell of dust and bitter coffee.

Chani leaned forward. “You must visit the southern gardens tomorrow.”

“I would be honored.”

Paul felt an invisible door close.

“we have duties.”

"Not everything is a duty," she said.

Sira placed her cup down and stared at Paul’s sightless eyes.

“The emperor should ask what he wants before deciding what everyone else must lose,” Paul said nothing. The future had given him no answers.
