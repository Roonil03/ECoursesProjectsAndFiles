By midnight, Platform 4 had become a museum of things nobody wanted: a single child’s mitten, a cracked umbrella, three newspapers softened by rain, and a yellow suitcase standing upright beside the bench.

The suitcase was covered in handwritten labels.

FOR EMERGENCIES.
FOR PEOPLE WHO HAVE FORGOTTEN HOW TO WAIT.
DO NOT OPEN UNLESS THE CLOCK IS WRONG.

A transit officer had already placed a notice beside it, but someone had drawn a neat red border around the paper and added, “This is not a solution.”

Across the platform, six paper cups had been arranged in a perfect semicircle, all facing the tunnel. Each cup contained a small object: a button, a marble, a dead battery, a feather, a brass key, and a folded receipt. Above them, taped to the tiled wall, was a sign that read:
TONIGHT’S PASSENGERS:
THE LATE ONE, THE LOUD ONE, THE LOST ONE, THE LIAR, THE SLEEPER, AND THE PERSON WHO WILL PRETEND NOT TO BE AFRAID.

Someone had underlined the late one seven times.

The electronic board announced another delay. A second note appeared beneath it:
THE TRAIN IS NOT LATE. IT IS THINKING.

Near the edge of the platform, a portable speaker faced the empty tunnel. Every thirty seconds, it played a recording of a calm voice saying, “Please remain exactly where you are.” Afterward came the sound of hurried footsteps, a door slamming, and a long theatrical sigh.

The officer unplugged it, but the speaker continued for another minute on its battery.

Inside the yellow suitcase were dozens of watches, all set to different times. Some had their hands removed. Others had been stopped at 11:11. Beneath them lay a stack of index cards, each describing an emergency that had never happened:

A woman drops her coffee.
A man checks his phone for the ninth time.
Someone begins to cry but changes their mind.

The final card was blank except for a command:
DO SOMETHING BEFORE IT IS TOO LATE.

At 12:17, the last train arrived. The platform emptied quickly, leaving the suitcase, the cups, and the red-bordered notice behind.

The officer gathered everything into a storage room, except for the brass key.

That remained on the bench, placed carefully beneath the station map, beside a note that said:
I WILL COME BACK FOR THIS.

The handwriting became smaller at the bottom.
EVENTUALLY.
