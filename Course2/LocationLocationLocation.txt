The rain began at midday.

Droplets clung to the copper rooftops of Vey. They slipped over the ornately carved stone surfaces of the buildings. Silver streams poured from the gutters into the busy streets below. On the eastern arched gateway where the city’s wall opened into the salt flats, a woman stood with her hands folded across a metal box.

Isla MacRae arrived from the landlocked republic of Orra. She had travelled far from the straight streets and buildings that did not slouch together like conspirators. She held a box that contained three measuring devices, some insulated cable and one letter sealed by the Ministry of Public Safety.

On the opposite side of the street, Ewan MacLeod stared down at the rain running off the rim of his well-worn red hat. Like his father, and grandfather, Ewan had grown up in Vey. His coat was patched on both elbows but the bright brass key hanging around his neck shone through the grey light.

Behind him, curled around a pile of empty grain boxes, was a boy.

Hamish Kerr had been on his way to deliver a message to the western watchtower when the storm hit. He had intended to wait five minutes but when Isla arrived, Ewan greeted her with a tense silence. Hamish slipped the message under his shirt and leaned in.

“The Ministry’s instruments recorded another pulse beneath the city.” The readings are higher than last year and if the chamber falls apart, the lower districts will be flooded with vapour.

Ewan brushed his hand against the bright brass key around his neck.

“The chamber has stood beneath Vey for six hundred years.”

"Old stone doesn’t make things any safer."

"It doesn’t. But we know how to read it."

Isla uncapped her box and reached inside. She pulled out a thin glass tube filled with blue liquid. The liquid shook inside the tube.

High on the end of the east street was a huge bell tower. It was aged and the bottom stones were a dark colour. No-one in Vey had climbed to the bell in living memory. Every child knew that the bell was created from the metal of a fallen star and it’s ringing prevented the sleeping river below from waking.

Isla looked up.

"Your council has granted me permission to enter the chamber beneath the tower."

Ewan frowned.

“The council granted you permission to inspect the entrance.”

"To check you need to enter."

"No."

"It says so."

The air smelled of damp iron.

Ewan pulled a rolled up piece of paper from his coat. It was different to Isla’s maps. It had been sewn together from scraps of old sail cloth and the lines were drawn using charcoal, salt and red thread.

“The chamber is not a mine.” This is listening chamber.

Isla studied the map. “You believe the city is listening to something?”

“I believe the city is being listened to.”

"To what?"

He glanced around at the busy streets. Women were carrying baskets over their heads. Old men placed bowls under leaking roofs. A child splashed through a puddle while his mother feigned a frown.

"Something that was here before our walls."

Isla sighed through her nose. In Orra, anything mysterious was given a number. “You understand that belief is not evidence.”

“That is not how the words were spoken.”

For the first time, Isla met his gaze. A strand of loose hair was dark with rain.

“The engineers told us the tunnel was stable. The Ministry told us the measurements were acceptable. They were wrong by three minutes.”

Ewan let the map drop.

Isla continued, “I am not here to insult your history. I am here because I have seen what happens when a city protects a story more carefully than it protects its people.”

Hamish leaned back against the boxes. He hadn’t really thought about it. He had assumed the outsider was just scared of Vey because she thought it was backwards. He hadn’t considered that the fear could have a source.

A bell rang.

Not the big one. The small one at the western watchpost.

Ewan’s head shot around. He clenched his hand around the brass key.

“That signal means the lower gate is open.”

Isla closed her box. “Then we have less time than you said.”

“I did not say we had time.”

They both walked outside into the rain. Ewan carried the old map and Isla carried the devices. They still disagreed.

Hamish waited until they were out of sight under the archway. He pulled the message from his shirt.

It was stamped with the council’s seal.

No-one should enter the bell chamber.

Underneath, in bright red ink, was written:

If the bell rings, wake the city.

Something knocked from deep beneath the ground.

He looked up at the tower...
And ran.
