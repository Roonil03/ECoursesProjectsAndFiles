As he ran through the ruined royal gardens, Elias saw a golden apple. It shone with a soft light, and when he took a bite, he felt a warm sensation spread through his body and tasted something sweet, like honey mixed with metal. He did not know what it was, but he knew the city bells were ringing. He had only a few minutes before the soldiers caught up with him.

Elias had been a bridge engineer in the city of Veyra for the last three years. He had always been brave when working with machinery, heights, and dangerous equipment, but he had never considered himself a fighter. That morning, the warlord had taken control of the central dam and threatened to flood the lower district if the people did not surrender. His little sister, Mira, was trapped there with hundreds of others. The only way to save them was to reach the dam and open the emergency gates.

He took another bite of the apple and felt another wave of warmth move through his body. However, he did not have much time to eat. He ran toward the dam as arrows struck the stone walls around him. One arrow hit his shoulder, but the wound was not too serious and soon stopped bleeding. Elias realized that the apple did not make him invincible; it simply made his injuries less severe. He continued running up the outside steps while hearing the soldiers chase him. Another arrow struck his leg, but he quickly pulled it out.

When he reached the dam’s control tower, he realized that it was locked from the inside. He panicked, picked up a fallen axe, and began striking the lock repeatedly. His hands became numb after so many blows. As he reached the top of the tower, the soldiers appeared, and the door suddenly swung open. Elias ran inside and saw three guards with their weapons drawn. One of them fired a crossbow, and the bolt struck Elias in the ribs. He fell against the wall, coughing, but strangely, he felt no pain.

Elias realized that the apple’s effects were beginning to fade, and he could feel time running out. He used all his strength to stand up and pulled the arrow from his side. Then he ran toward the nearest guard and pushed him into the control panel. The impact broke two levers, and water roared through the partially opened gates. The tower shook violently as the dam struggled under the pressure.

Elias crawled toward the emergency wheel and realized that he was surrounded by the remaining guards. One of them kicked him hard in the ribs while another raised a sword. Using all his willpower, Elias grabbed the sword with both hands. Despite the pain as the blade cut into his palms, he pushed the guard backward. Then, using the last of his strength, he turned the emergency wheel. The gates opened fully, diverting the flood away from the lower district.

The captain of the guards grabbed Elias by the collar and threw him against the wall.

“You have doomed the city,” the captain said.

“No, I saved it,” Elias replied, looking out of the window. The water was flowing safely into the empty canal. “I saved the people.”

The captain raised his sword, but another bell suddenly sounded. Mira and the rescued citizens rushed into the control tower from below, carrying tools, ropes, and stones.

The captain looked at them and then at Elias. He hesitated before finally lowering his sword.

As soon as the apple lost its power, Elias collapsed into Mira’s arms. He had saved the people, but he would be unable to work for several months. When people asked why he had risked his life, he told them that the apple had not made him brave; it had simply given him enough time to be brave.

Years passed, and although the apple was gone, Elias kept its stem in a wooden box. It reminded him that even temporary power could make a lasting difference. He often told the story to Mira and the people of Veyra, reminding them that courage is valuable.

Many people came to him for advice, and he would tell them, “Bravery does not always mean being fearless; it means choosing to act even when you feel weak and vulnerable.”

His story inspired many people in the community to find their inner courage. He had not only saved his sister and the people of Veyra, but had also inspired countless others.

The golden apple had taught Elias a valuable lesson: courage can develop during times of crisis. Whenever he looked at the wooden box containing the stem, he smiled, remembering how a few simple decisions had led to extraordinary results.

Knowing that true bravery comes from within, Elias dedicated his life to helping those in need. The legacy of the golden apple continued, and its magic lived on through the courage of many.
