Sarah wanted the brass moose from the abandoned fairground, though she had never admitted why. To reach it, she used a trick, telling the night guard that her little brother was trapped inside. Her weakness was a paralyzing fear of heights, worsened by every ladder she saw. A childhood memory returned: her father laughing as she froze halfway up a tree. Still, the moose mattered because it was the last object her missing father had ever promised her. She climbed aboard the rusted Ferris wheel, gripping the seat until her knuckles whitened. The wheel groaned beneath her, and the guard began shouting from the locked entrance. A painted tiger on the ticket booth seemed to glare as the wind pushed her higher. Sarah told herself she could pretend the empty fairground was only a dream. Then the wheel jolted, throwing her against the safety bar and snapping one of its bolts. Below, a paper carrot skittered across the dirt, pointing toward the control shed. The wheel stopped with Sarah dangling above the highest platform, while the guard fetched a ladder. She spotted the shed’s broken door and realized the emergency controls might still work. Inside, an ancient appliance sparked when she kicked its power switch. The Ferris wheel lurched backwards, swinging her seat toward the maintenance platform. Sara jumped, landed badly, and crawled beneath a bent metal cage. The guard reached the platform just as the wheel began spinning again. From the tower, a warning bell rings, and the fairground lights flicker awake. Sarah saw the brass moose hanging from a crane above the central ring. A black crow burst from the crane, startling her toward the edge. Her fear rose like a wall, but so did the memory of her father’s promise. She grabbed the moose’s chain, pulled with both hands, and refused to look down. The crane swung loose, and the entire fairground seemed ready to explode beneath her. Sarah hugged the brass moose tightly as the guard finally hauled them both to safety.
